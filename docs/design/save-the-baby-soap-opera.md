# Save The Baby: The Fortune He Claimed

A serial drama for the game. New supporting names and the fictional royal house below are creative additions. This manuscript preserves the creator's supplied foundation; it has not yet replaced the playable story files.

## The promise

Victor believes every good thing that happens near him belongs to him. Amara once mistook that certainty for confidence. Now her baby is missing, the man she suspects is smiling, and the man she loves must learn that a person cannot be rescued by cleverness alone.

The audience's central question is not merely where Ethan is. It is: **who has decided that Amara's child is worth less than their pride?**

## Before the rain

Before there was an inheritance, there was a folding table beside a busy road.

Amara sold food there in the morning, delivered parcels in the afternoon and repaired clothes after dark. Sometimes she counted the day's money twice, hoping the second count would leave enough for herself after she paid the rent she shared with Victor.

Victor called it their season of preparation.

"Great men are not always recognised early," he told her.

When a customer paid generously, he called it his aura. When Amara found another job, he called it his blessing. When she worked until her fingers hurt, he asked why she looked so tired after such a lucky day.

One evening she placed a new pair of shoes before him.

**VICTOR:** You see? Things change when you stay close to me.

**AMARA:** I saved for six weeks.

**VICTOR:** And who brought those customers into your life?

She looked at the shoes. She had imagined that he would thank her.

Then Amara met Adrian, the son of a wealthy royal family from another community. His car broke down near her stall. She gave his driver water before she knew who was sitting in the back.

Adrian returned without the car the next day.

He came again. He learned when she opened, which supplier cheated her and how she hid the best chair whenever an elderly customer arrived. When he spoke of changing her life, she asked whether he had noticed that she was already trying to change it herself.

He laughed. Then he apologised.

Months later, he asked her to marry him.

Amara went home and told Victor everything.

For a moment Victor said nothing. Then he smiled.

**VICTOR:** You finally understand what my aura can do.

**AMARA:** A man has asked to marry me, and that is what you heard?

**VICTOR:** Say yes. Let him spend. Let him open the doors. When we have enough, you come back to me.

**AMARA:** And what do I tell him?

**VICTOR:** Whatever a rich fool needs to hear.

Amara removed the coins she had brought home from her pocket and put them on the table.

**AMARA:** Those are for this week's rent. There will not be another week with me.

She left Victor before accepting Adrian. Her love was not a scheme they shared.

Adrian's family refused the marriage. His mother, Lady Orena, would not receive Amara at the family residence. His cousin, Regent Kellan, spoke as though a poor woman's presence could stain the royal name.

Adrian promised that they would come around.

They did not.

When Amara became pregnant, the family called her calculating. When Ethan was born, they questioned his place in the family before they asked whether he was healthy.

**ORENA:** A child born to a pauper from another community will not inherit this throne.

**ADRIAN:** He has inherited my name. You do not have to give him your affection to make him my son.

Adrian acknowledged Ethan and arranged for a portion of his personal wealth to pass to him. The royal succession and that private provision were different disputes; his family intended to fight both.

Before either dispute could be settled, Adrian died.

The official announcement said it was sudden. The family said it was private. Amara was told when she could mourn, where she could stand and which questions were inappropriate.

Victor sent flowers.

On the card he wrote: **You know who was here before all of this.**

Amara began to wonder whether he had done more than wait for Adrian to disappear. She had no proof. She could not even say the suspicion aloud without wondering what it would cost her son.

The provision Adrian left for Ethan gave them a home and security. It did not make Amara the owner of the royal fortune, and it did not make Ethan an accepted heir.

Victor arrived at her new gate with the confidence of a man coming to collect something.

**VICTOR:** We can start again.

**AMARA:** I would rather remain unmarried for the rest of my life.

**VICTOR:** You think the money changed you.

**AMARA:** No. I finally listened when you told me who you were.

Later, David entered her life through an ordinary problem: the website for Amara's small business kept losing customer orders. He repaired it, explained what had happened and refused to pretend that the repair was magic.

They became friends before they became lovers. David listened when she spoke about Adrian. He learned which cup Ethan liked and that Amara checked the nursery door twice every night.

For the first time since Adrian's death, people heard her laugh without immediately lowering their voices.

Victor heard about it too.

**VICTOR:** So you can love another man. Just not me.

**AMARA:** That is what I have been telling you.

**VICTOR:** Then share the money. My aura brought it. If I cannot have the marriage, I will take my part of the fortune.

She closed the gate.

That was three days before the rain.

## Episode 1 — The House That Never Opened

Rain wakes Amara before dawn. Ethan's feeding cup is beside her bed. She reaches for it, listening for the small impatient sound that usually follows.

The sound does not come.

From the nursery doorway she sees Victor, his face briefly lit by lightning, carrying Ethan toward the rear passage. She says his name. He turns. She sees her son's blue sleeve against his coat.

The lights fail.

Amara runs after them. A door slams. She finds an empty passage.

The front gate is locked. The back door is bolted. The windows are secured. Ethan's blanket lies beside the cot.

David arrives while she is still holding it.

**AMARA:** I saw him. Don't tell me I dreamed him.

**DAVID:** I believe that you saw Victor. Tell me where he was standing.

**AMARA:** You repair things nobody else understands. Please repair this.

David cannot answer.

**AMARA:** Bring Ethan back. Please, David. Save the baby. Whatever happens, bring him back to me alive.

Her phone rings. Victor speaks before she can say his name.

**VICTOR:** My money or Ethan.

**AMARA:** He is not your son. That money is not yours.

**VICTOR:** Then decide which one you want to keep.

The call ends. David finds that his attempt to record it started too late. They have Amara's account, a call notification and a locked house. They do not yet have Victor's location or proof of how he entered.

The first interactive choice appears:

- **Call Detective Morris.** Start an organised search and preserve the scene. Police activity may be visible to someone watching the house.
- **Trace the message with Amara.** David examines the original message and account activity; Amara identifies the people and routines behind the records. Technical evidence may be planted.
- **Follow the rear passage yourself.** David pursues a physical lead immediately, sending Amara his location. He has no nearby backup if it is a trap.

The routes change what is learned first and who is present. They do not make the other characters disappear from existence, and the player may later request help.

**Cliffhanger:** David checks the household access record. The last authorised entry is Amara's own credential—used while she was asleep.

## Episode 2 — Her Name at the Door

Morris takes the disappearance seriously and begins a search. Amara does not need to prove a completed criminal case before anyone looks for Ethan. What Morris cannot yet establish is where Victor is, who helped him, or how the locked house was used.

On the police route, his team preserves the household records. On the signal route, David first finds conflicting timestamps. On the solo route, he discovers an old passage entrance and must stop himself from entering an unsafe space alone. Each discovery exposes the weakness of the other clues.

The credential used that morning belongs to a household service arrangement Adrian established. Someone has reused Amara's authorisation.

**AMARA:** They have made my name open the door for the man I shut out.

**MORRIS:** A name in a record is not the same thing as the person using it.

At the end of the episode, the team secures the house and removes the unauthorised access. This is a permanent victory: Victor cannot use that route again.

**Cliffhanger:** A payment attached to the service account came from Adrian's estate office after Adrian died.

## Episode 3 — The Prince's Signature

Amara demands entry to the estate office. Lady Orena offers to help find Ethan if Amara will let the family manage his affairs.

**AMARA:** You would not say his name when he was born. Say it now.

Orena says it: Ethan. For a moment, grief breaks through her composure. Then Kellan interrupts.

An estate clerk, Nessa, quietly provides a payment copy she was told to destroy. It establishes an actual link between the household service and a vehicle contractor. It does not identify Adrian's killer or prove Orena ordered the kidnapping.

**Cliffhanger:** Amara recognises Victor in an old photograph taken inside Adrian's estate office. Victor had claimed never to have met him.

## Episode 4 — A Man Who Was Already There

David confronts Victor remotely with the photograph. Victor admits that he once arranged an introduction to one of Adrian's associates. He inflates that small contact into ownership of Adrian's whole relationship with Amara.

**VICTOR:** Without me, none of you would have met.

**DAVID:** Knowing where a road begins does not make every person on it yours.

The team finds that the first footprints were staged. A recovered fingerprint belongs to a worker who was not at the house. Morris establishes that the traces were planted; the investigation stops blaming an innocent man.

The evidence still yields something useful: the planted material passed through the same contractor identified by Nessa. David's initial theory was wrong, but correcting it protects someone and narrows the search.

**Cliffhanger:** Nessa calls. Someone has discovered that she copied the payment—and her younger brother has not returned from work.

## Episode 5 — The Price of Speaking

This time the heroes arrive in time. Depending on the chosen approach, Morris coordinates a safe interception, David spots a false collection message, or the player follows a witness to the contractor's yard. Nessa's brother is found alive and brought to safety.

He heard Ethan crying in a nearby vehicle. It was not a recording. His statement gives the search its first independent sighting of the baby that morning.

**AMARA:** Did someone pick him up when he cried?

**WITNESS:** Yes. A woman. She held him until he stopped.

It is not the answer Amara expected to need most.

**Cliffhanger:** The woman wore the estate staff's green pin.

## Episode 6 — The Woman in the Car

The witness identifies Lami, a former estate attendant. Lami believed she was helping move Adrian's child out of danger while a family dispute was settled. When she discovered Victor's involvement, she tried to leave a warning.

She is accountable for taking part; her fear does not erase that. But her cooperation saves time. She gives the team a current destination and confirms that Victor is asking for the inheritance.

Amara is allowed to be angry without the scene making her cruel.

**LAMI:** I thought the family had agreed.

**AMARA:** I am his family. Did you ask me?

**Cliffhanger:** Lami says the arrangement was approved by Kellan. Before she can finish, Victor sends a new demand: Amara must come without David.

## Episode 7 — What Love Will Not Ask

Amara considers obeying. David tries to stop her by deciding what she should do. Their first serious argument is not about whether they love each other; it is about who gets to make a frightened mother's choices.

**AMARA:** Do not save me from Victor by becoming another man who decides for me.

**DAVID:** I am afraid you will go through that door and I will lose both of you.

They make a plan together. Amara will speak to Victor. David and Morris will work from what she shares. On an initially solo route, this is a deliberate opportunity to bring police into the search, not a silent rewrite of the player's history.

Amara draws Victor into talking about the morning. His knowledge matches details never made public. Morris now has corroboration beyond a disputed identification.

**Cliffhanger:** Victor says, "Ask your rich man's family why they paid for the car."

## Episode 8 — A Family Meeting

Kellan claims the payment was for routine estate business. Nessa supplies the second half of the record: he described Amara as unfit and authorised a private transfer of Ethan's care without consulting her.

Kellan wanted the child removed from Amara's influence and hidden from the succession dispute. Victor used the arrangement to turn Ethan into ransom. The two men cooperated, then lost control of each other. Neither fact excuses the other.

Orena learns that Kellan acted in the family's name. Her first instinct is to protect the royal house from scandal. Amara refuses that bargain.

**AMARA:** If silence is the price of your help, keep it. My son has paid enough for this family's reputation.

Orena releases the contractor records. It is a useful action, not instant forgiveness.

**Cliffhanger:** A newly recovered message shows Kellan also arranged a private meeting on the night Adrian died.

## Episode 9 — The Dead Man's Voice

Adrian left an unfinished voice note after arguing with Kellan about Ethan's future. The note confirms that Adrian intended to stand by his son. It does not reveal how he died.

For the first time since the kidnapping, Amara hears Adrian's voice without Victor's interruption.

**ADRIAN'S RECORDING:** If they tell you I changed my mind about our boy, ask them to let you hear me say it.

David stays beside her while she listens. His place in her life does not depend on erasing the man she lost.

The contractor records and Lami's account identify a riverside service compound. Morris closes the usable road exits while David compares the current layout with the old one. They choose the location through corroborating clues, not through one miraculous hack.

**Cliffhanger:** A storm alert announces that the compound's lower service area will flood. The exit Victor expects to use will soon be underwater.

## Episode 10 — No More Empty Rooms

The team reaches the compound while Victor is still there. The player's earlier route affects the advantage: earlier police contact gives a better-established perimeter, technical investigation identifies a working alarm circuit, and physical scouting reveals a safe access path. None guarantees an effortless rescue.

David finds Ethan's feeding cup. This time it is not the reward for arriving late. Ethan cries from the next room.

David reaches him and confirms to Amara that he is alive.

Victor blocks the way out.

**VICTOR:** Look at you. The hero. Do you think she will love you without that child in your arms?

**DAVID:** Move away from the door.

David does not debate the aura. Ethan is frightened. The storm is rising. The argument has become smaller than the life in his arms.

**Cliffhanger:** The lights fail, and the service gate begins to close between David and the rescuers.

## Episode 11 — The Door We Hold

Amara, Morris and David solve different parts of the final obstruction. Amara remembers a manual release described in Adrian's household documents; David identifies which control still has power; Morris's team reaches the mechanism from outside.

The match-three objectives make these actions legible: clear the blocking path, restore the control circuit, secure the exit. The game does not announce that Ethan is rescued after each intermediate board.

Victor attempts to trade the release for a promise that Amara will return to him.

**AMARA:** You are not asking for love. You are asking me to call this love.

The gate stops. David carries Ethan through. Morris's team secures Victor.

Amara takes her son. She does not deliver a speech. She checks his face, touches his hands and calls his name until he responds. The game gives this moment room. A medical check follows; immediate reunion is not used as proof that nothing happened to him.

**AMARA, TO DAVID:** You brought him back.

**DAVID:** We brought him back.

**Cliffhanger:** At the estate, Kellan finds the original letter Nessa copied. One paragraph is missing from the version Amara received.

## Episode 12 — Alive Is Only the Beginning

Ethan returns home. Amara replaces the nursery lock and leaves the door open while she sits beside his cot. She and David are exhausted, relieved and changed. Neither pretends that one rescue repairs everything.

Victor faces consequences. Kellan's part in the transfer is under investigation. Orena asks to see her grandson.

**AMARA:** You may ask. You will not order.

The first season fulfils the game's promise: Ethan has been found alive. The next season begins with a different threat—the attempt to take his future through the estate and the unanswered death of his father. The baby is not immediately kidnapped again.

Nessa brings Amara the missing paragraph. Adrian had written that he feared someone close to the family was using his name to move money. He intended to speak to an investigator.

There is no signature naming a murderer. There is a date, a meeting and a person who had claimed never to be present.

David looks from the letter to Amara.

**DAVID:** Do you want me to start looking tonight?

Amara listens to Ethan breathing in the next room.

**AMARA:** Tonight, we stay here. Tomorrow, we find out who took his father from him.

**End of the first rescue season. The family story continues.**

## How the game tells this story

- Each episode opens with a concrete problem, earns a discovery through several puzzle stages, and ends with a changed situation. Not every stage needs a conversation.
- A completed level grants progress the story keeps: an innocent person cleared, an exit secured, a witness protected, a current destination established. Victor does not erase every victory.
- Footprints and fingerprints are leads with sources, not infallible answers. Victor's board-clearing ability becomes a readable interference mechanic; it cannot retroactively erase a clue the player already earned.
- Locks, tape, hazards and restored pictures belong to particular scenes: a sealed record, a damaged image, a blocked service corridor. Restoring an image reveals a missing detail; it is not a second unrelated story.
- Failed attempts do not injure Ethan, kill a witness, advance the kidnapper's escape or alter the plot. The player retries the same problem. Threats are character dialogue, not a punishment for puzzle difficulty.
- Police, signal and solo approaches supply different information and relationships before converging. Switching approaches is spoken and acknowledged. Police arrival is never silently inserted into a route that excluded them.
- Ethan is an infant. He does not leave written instructions, deliberate coded drawings or adult deductions. His sounds, belongings and care routine can provide credible clues.
- Cooperation between players can be framed as the whole community supporting the search. Do not promise shared live evidence or multiplayer features that the game does not implement.
- Short version of the invitation: **"Amara has asked David for help. David needs every clue. Join the search. Save the baby."**

## Continuity boundaries for adaptation

Victor's aura is his self-serving belief, not a supernatural fact the narration confirms. Amara genuinely chooses Adrian after leaving Victor; she does not carry out Victor's proposed fraud. Adrian acknowledges Ethan, but his family rejects the child. The provision comes from Adrian to Ethan; Victor has no paternal or ownership claim. Amara truly sees Victor taking Ethan; the mystery concerns access and assistance, not a reversal that calls her delusional. Adrian's death remains unexplained in the first season. Kellan and Orena must not be treated as interchangeable, and helping the rescue does not erase earlier harm. The final rescue is real, not another recording, dream or false room.
